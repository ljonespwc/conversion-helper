# Typing... Series — Social Media Posting Strategy

## Posting Cadence

### X (Twitter)
- **3-4x per week** — X rewards frequency and recency. The feed moves fast.
- Best times: Tuesday-Thursday, 8-10 AM and 12-1 PM (B2B audience lunch scroll)
- Use compact (16:9) for most posts. Extended (4:3) for 3+ message conversations that need room.

### LinkedIn
- **2x per week** — LinkedIn penalizes over-posting. Quality > quantity.
- Best times: Tuesday-Thursday, 7-9 AM (morning commute scroll)
- Use compact (1.91:1) as default. It's the native LinkedIn image format so it gets maximum feed real estate without looking off.

## Content Strategy

### Batch by theme, don't dump randomly

- **Monday:** "The Late Night Shopper" — 2-3 AM conversations, impulse buyers
- **Wednesday:** "The Overthinker" — long questions, objection handling
- **Friday:** "The One-Word Wonder" — short punchy exchanges, lighter tone for end of week

This creates anticipation. People start associating days with archetypes.

### Rotate taglines weekly
Not per post. Let one tagline build recognition for a few posts before switching.

## Post Copy

Keep it short. The card does the heavy lifting. The post text is just the hook.

### X format
```
Typing... | Vol. 31

It's 1:44 AM. Someone has a question about a true crime subscription box.

The AI didn't flinch.

#EasyAsk #AI #ConversationalAI
```

### LinkedIn format (longer, more narrative)
```
This is what happens when someone lands on your website at 1:44 AM with a question.

No support team awake. No chatbot menu tree. Just an AI that actually knows your product.

Vol. 31 of Typing... — real conversations powered by EasyAsk.

→ What questions are YOUR visitors asking when no one's watching?
```

LinkedIn rewards engagement, so end with a question or call-to-reflection.

## Growth Tactics

### 1. Cross-post strategically, not identically
Same card, different post copy. X gets the punchy version, LinkedIn gets the story version. Never identical text — algorithms can detect it and both platforms deprioritize reposts.

### 2. Thread on X, carousel on LinkedIn
Every 2 weeks, bundle 3-4 related volumes into:
- **X:** A thread — "The best late-night questions we've seen this month"
- **LinkedIn:** A document/carousel post (PDF upload) — gets 2-3x the reach of single images

### 3. Tag verticals
When the example is about a candle brand, tag candle/DTC hashtags. When it's a SaaS example, tag SaaS hashtags. This pulls in niche audiences who see themselves in the scenario.

### 4. Engagement bait (tasteful)
Every 5th post, flip the script:
- "What would YOUR AI say to this question? Drop your answer below."
- "Rate this AI response 1-10"
- "What's the weirdest question someone's asked on YOUR website?"

### 5. Repost winners
Track which volumes get the most engagement. Repost them 3-4 weeks later with fresh copy. Most of your audience didn't see it the first time.

## Metrics to Watch

- **X:** Impressions, bookmark rate (people saving it = high signal), link clicks to profile
- **LinkedIn:** Impressions, comment count (LinkedIn's algorithm weighs comments heavily), profile views

## Realistic Timeline

- **Weeks 1-4:** Post consistently, build the visual identity. Low engagement is normal.
- **Weeks 4-8:** Engagement picks up as people recognize the format. Start threading/carouseling.
- **Weeks 8-12:** If a post pops off, lean into that archetype/vertical. Double down on what works.

## Key Principle

The card format is the brand. People should be able to recognize a Typing... post from the thumbnail alone before reading a word. The dark background + amber accent + consistent layout does this. Don't change the visual identity once you start posting.
