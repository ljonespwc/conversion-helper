# Typing... — Marketing Content Brief

**Series Name:** Typing...
**Display format:** `Typing... | Vol. 14` (volume number increments with each post)
**Channels:** X (Twitter), LinkedIn (V1) — TikTok (V2)
**Cadence:** Daily (or 3-5x/week)
**Goal:** Build brand awareness for EasyAsk across verticals by showcasing funny, relatable, fictional chat exchanges that demonstrate what the product does — without ever using real customer data.
**Branding note:** The series name "Typing..." contains no reference to EasyAsk. EasyAsk appears only in the per-post CTA tagline at the bottom of each image. The series itself is the brand.

---

## Concept

**Typing...** is a daily social content series featuring a short, fictional chat exchange between a website visitor and an EasyAsk-powered widget. Each post is set in a different business vertical and page context, showing that real humans ask weird, terse, hilarious, and surprisingly deep questions — and EasyAsk handles every one of them with composure.

The humor is never mean. The AI never punches down. The comedy comes from the *contrast* between chaotic human energy and calm, competent AI energy.

## Post Anatomy

Every Typing... post includes these elements:

### 1. Header / Context Card
- **Business type** — The more specific, the funnier. Not "restaurant" but "Family-owned dumpling shop in Queens." Not "SaaS" but "Project management tool for freelance clowns."
- **Page** — Where on the site this happened (Pricing page, About Us, Product detail page, FAQ, etc.)
- **Day & Time** — The timestamp tells its own joke. Tuesday 2:14 AM hits different than Monday 9:01 AM.

### 2. Setup Line
One sentence that frames the scene. Written like a nature documentary narrator observing human behavior in the wild.

Examples:
- "A visitor on a luxury mattress site has been browsing since midnight. They have questions."
- "Someone found the FAQ page for a $6/month budgeting app. They're treating it like a Senate hearing."
- "A first-time visitor to an artisanal hot sauce website gets right to the point."

### 3. The Exchange
1 to 3 question-and-answer pairs styled as chat bubbles. This is the core content.

**The human** is funny by being:
- Absurdly terse ("price?" / "Spanish?" / "works?")
- Wildly off-topic or on the wrong website
- Unreasonably demanding or thorough for the context
- Hilariously specific about something trivial
- Way too honest about their situation
- Asking at 3 AM on a Tuesday
- Treating a $12 purchase like a life-altering commitment
- Clearly multitasking / distracted / half-asleep

**The EasyAsk AI** is funny by being:
- Unfailingly helpful no matter what
- Impressively thorough about absurd questions
- Earnestly on-topic when the human clearly isn't
- Finding a real answer where there shouldn't be one
- Gently steering chaos back to something useful
- Warm, specific, and competent — the comedy is in the composure

### 4. "What the AI Really Wanted to Say" (Optional Layer)
A short italicized aside — the AI's inner monologue. Think sitcom aside or nature documentary voice, not roast comedy. This is relatable, not snarky.

Rules for this section:
- Never insults the visitor
- Never discourages them from buying
- Can express mild bewilderment, affection, or existential reflection
- Should feel like a coworker turning to camera on The Office
- One sentence, max two

Examples:
- *"It's 3 AM and they're comparison-shopping hot sauce. I respect the commitment."*
- *"They asked one word. I wrote four paragraphs. This is fine."*
- *"They're on the pricing page for the third time today. We're in a relationship now."*

## Recurring Visitor Archetypes

These aren't named characters, but recognizable patterns that audiences will start to anticipate and enjoy. Tag them with a small label in the post (e.g., "[The 3 AM Shopper]").

### The 3 AM Shopper
Always browsing at ungodly hours. Questions reveal insomnia-fueled decision-making, existential purchasing, or someone in a very different timezone. Their commitment is unquestionable.

### One-Word Wonder
Communicates exclusively in 1-3 word messages. Forces the AI to interpret and deliver. "Price?" "Spanish?" "Refund?" The AI responds with comprehensive paragraphs. Neither party sees a problem with this dynamic.

### The Overthinker
Treating a $9/month subscription or a $15 product like they're buying a house. Wants to know the pros and cons, the return policy, compatibility with their lifestyle, and whether it aligns with their values. For a candle.

### Wrong Website Entirely
Clearly looking for something else. Landed here by accident or confusion. The AI does its best to help anyway, which somehow makes it funnier.

### The Negotiator
Always fishing for a discount, a code, a deal, a hookup. Regardless of what the product is or what page they're on. Will try multiple angles in the same conversation.

### The Loyal Regular
References past visits to this fictional business's site. Has opinions. Knows the product line. Treats the AI like a familiar bartender. May have an emotional attachment to the brand that slightly exceeds what's warranted.

### The Multitasker
Clearly doing 10 things at once. Messages arrive in fragments. Context shifts mid-conversation. May paste in text from a completely different tab. The AI rolls with it.

### The Panicked Parent
A parent in a time-pressured, kid-related emergency — science fair project due tomorrow, birthday party in 3 hours, etc. The urgency is real, the planning was not. The AI becomes the calm adult in the room.

### The Philosopher
Asks questions that are technically about the product but veer into unexpectedly deep territory. "But do I *need* a second monitor, or do I just *want* one?" The AI stays grounded in product content but the exchange radiates existential energy.

### The Honesty Test
The AI honestly admits it doesn't have the answer — and the visitor's reaction is the comedy. Maybe they're relieved ("finally, an honest AI"), maybe they're weirdly grateful, maybe they double down and try to rephrase like that'll unlock hidden knowledge, or maybe they just accept it with a calmness that's funnier than the question. The AI redirects gracefully — suggests contacting support, offers what it *does* know, or reframes toward something it can help with. **This archetype is a key brand differentiator: EasyAsk doesn't hallucinate.** Every one of these posts is quietly saying "other AI chatbots would have made something up here."

Visitor reaction subtypes:
- **The Impressed One:** "Wait, you're an AI and you're admitting you don't know? I respect that."
- **The Rephraser:** Asks the same question three different ways, convinced the answer is hiding in there somewhere.
- **The Zen Acceptor:** Takes the "I don't have that info" with a shrug and a "fair enough" that's weirdly zen.
- **The Oversharer:** Responds to "I don't have that" by unloading the full context of why they needed the answer, turning a dead-end into a therapy session.
- **The Converter:** The honesty actually builds enough trust that they buy/sign up anyway. "Well if you're not gonna BS me, I'll give it a shot."

## Channel Formatting

### X (Twitter)
- **Format:** Single image post (the chat mockup) + text caption
- **Caption:** The setup line + tagline. Keep it under 200 characters so the image does the work.
- **Image:** Styled to look like an EasyAsk widget chat window. Orange gradient header, chat bubbles, business/page context visible at top. Clean, readable at mobile size.
- **Alt text:** Always include — full transcript of the exchange for accessibility.
- **Thread option:** On the best-performing posts, reply with "What the AI really wanted to say" as a follow-up tweet for bonus engagement.

### LinkedIn
- **Format:** Image post (same chat mockup) + longer caption
- **Caption structure:**
  1. Hook line (the setup — grab attention in the first line before "see more")
  2. Brief context about the vertical/scenario (1-2 sentences)
  3. "What the AI really wanted to say" included inline
  4. Tagline
  5. Soft CTA: "What's the weirdest question your site visitors ask?" (engagement bait that's actually relevant)
- **Hashtags:** #EasyAsk #AIChat #CustomerExperience #ConversationalAI (keep it to 3-5, relevant ones)
- **Carousel option:** Weekly roundup — "Best of This Week's Chats" as a 5-slide carousel on Fridays

## Tone & Voice Guidelines

### The Visitor (Human)
- Casual, unfiltered, sometimes barely literate
- Never mean or offensive — just funny, relatable, very human
- Their questions should make people think "I've asked something like that" or "I've gotten that question from a customer"
- Typos are OK when they add character (not every time)

### The AI (EasyAsk)
- Warm, helpful, specific, thorough
- Pulls from "the content" — always sounds grounded, never generic
- Uses bullet points, specific numbers, concrete details when appropriate
- Asks a relevant follow-up question at the end (mirrors real EasyAsk behavior)
- Never snarky, never condescending, never robotic
- The humor comes from its competence in absurd situations, not from attitude

### "What the AI Really Wanted to Say"
- First person, italicized
- One sentence, max two
- Relatable inner monologue — mild bewilderment, quiet pride, gentle existential reflection
- Think: the calmest person in a chaotic room, turning to camera
- Never mean, never discouraging, never breaks the helpful persona

## Content Rules

1. **Never use real customer data.** All chats are fictional. Inspired by real patterns, never sourced from actual conversations.
2. **Never make fun of the visitor in a way that would make a business owner uncomfortable.** The visitor is the hero of every joke — relatable, not ridiculous.
3. **Span verticals widely.** The series should cover: e-commerce, SaaS, professional services, restaurants, health/fitness, education, luxury goods, home services, creative businesses, niche/weird products, B2B, etc.
4. **Keep exchanges short.** 1-3 Q&A pairs max. The constraint is the comedy. Don't over-explain the joke.
5. **The AI should always look good.** Every post is a soft product demo. Someone watching should think: "I want that on my site."
6. **Timestamps matter.** Use them for comedic effect. 3 AM purchases, Monday 8:59 AM "I haven't had coffee yet" energy, Friday 4:55 PM "checking out early" vibes.

## TikTok Formatting (V2)

TikTok is a different animal from X and LinkedIn. The content translates well but the delivery needs to be native to the platform. **Note: TikTok is V2 — not included in the initial screenshot tool or launch cadence.**

### Format: Vertical Video (9:16), 15-45 seconds

**Structure:**
1. **Hook (first 2 seconds):** Text overlay or voiceover that sets the scene. "A customer on a luxury mattress site at 3 AM..." — this is the setup line from the brief, spoken or shown as text.
2. **The chat (5-20 seconds):** Screen-record or animated scroll of the widget mockup. Messages appear one at a time with a slight delay — mimicking real chat. Use the screenshot tool output as the visual base. Subtle typing sounds or message "pop" sounds for each bubble.
3. **The punchline (3-5 seconds):** "What the AI really wanted to say" appears as a text overlay or is read aloud. This is the viral moment — the screenshot people share.
4. **Tag (2 seconds):** Tagline + EasyAsk logo or handle.

**Audio options:**
- **Voiceover:** Read the setup and "what the AI wanted to say" lines. Dry, deadpan delivery works best. Don't read the chat messages — let people read those on screen.
- **Trending sounds:** When a trending audio fits (and it will — "oh no" sounds, dramatic piano, the "he's a 10 but..." format), use it. The chat content is modular enough to slot into almost any trending template.
- **No audio:** Pure text + chat mockup + captions. Works well and is easier to produce at volume.

**Captions:** Always on. Most TikTok is watched on mute.

## Viral Strategy

### The Core Mechanic: Recognition Comedy

The reason this format has viral potential is **recognition comedy** — the audience sees themselves or their customers in the content and feels compelled to share it. "This is literally my customers" is the reaction we're engineering. Every share is an implicit endorsement: the person sharing is saying "I relate to this" while exposing their audience to EasyAsk.

---

### Strategy 1: Industry Callout Series

**How it works:** Dedicate specific days or weeks to specific verticals. "Restaurant Week," "SaaS Founders Week," "Wedding Vendors Week." Tag the post with the industry.

**Why it goes viral:** People in that industry tag their coworkers, friends, competitors. A wedding photographer sees the "my cousin has a nice camera" post and tags every photographer they know. A SaaS founder sees the spreadsheet comparison post and quote-tweets it with "I'm in this photo." The content self-distributes through professional communities.

**Execution:**
- X: "It's Restaurant Week on Typing.... If you've ever worked in food service, this one's for you."
- LinkedIn: Tag relevant industry hashtags. The industry-specific posts will get picked up by niche LinkedIn communities.
- TikTok: "POV: You work at a bakery and your website chat goes off at 11 PM." Use the POV format — it's native to TikTok and drives comments.

---

### Strategy 2: Audience Participation / UGC Engine

**How it works:** Invite the audience to submit their own real (anonymized) customer questions. "Send us the weirdest question a customer has ever asked on your website. We'll turn it into a Typing...."

**Why it goes viral:** People LOVE sharing their weird customer stories. It's a genre unto itself (see: r/talesfromretail, client horror story threads, "Clients from Hell"). By giving them a structured, branded outlet, EasyAsk becomes the hub for this content. Every submission is a potential post, and every submitter will share it when it goes live.

**Execution:**
- X: Pin a tweet — "What's the weirdest question someone has asked on your website? Reply or DM us. Best ones become a Typing...." Repost the best replies with the mockup treatment.
- LinkedIn: "Business owners: what's the one question your customers keep asking that makes you sigh? Drop it below." This is engagement bait that actually serves the brand.
- TikTok: "Stitch this with the weirdest customer question you've ever gotten." Stitch/duet formats are TikTok's most viral mechanic. People film their reaction to the chat, adding their own story.

**Bonus flywheel:** The submissions double as market research. You learn what real businesses struggle with, which informs both future content AND product messaging.

---

### Strategy 3: "Wrong Website" as a Standalone Series

**How it works:** The "Wrong Website Entirely" archetype gets its own mini-series. "When Someone Finds Your Chat Widget But They're Looking for Something Completely Different."

**Why it goes viral:** It's universally relatable — everyone has been on the wrong website, asked a dumb question to the wrong person, or received a wildly off-topic customer inquiry. The AI's graceful handling of it is the punchline. It's absurdist humor with a product demo baked in.

**Execution:**
- X: Thread format — "Wrong Website Wednesday: a weekly thread of people asking the completely wrong business the completely wrong question. The AI does its best."
- TikTok: This is the strongest TikTok format. Quick cuts between increasingly absurd questions hitting increasingly unrelated businesses. "Someone asked an HVAC company about orchids." Stack 3-4 rapid-fire wrong-website moments in one 30-second video. The speed and absurdity drives rewatches (which the algorithm loves).

---

### Strategy 4: The 3 AM Compilation

**How it works:** Monthly or weekly compilation of all the 3 AM Shopper posts. "Everything people asked AI chatbots at 3 AM this month."

**Why it goes viral:** The "late-night internet behavior" genre is massive. People recognize themselves. It's confessional content that feels harmless and funny. The 3 AM timestamp is doing half the comedy work already.

**Execution:**
- X: Thread or carousel of 5-7 3 AM chats. "A thread of things people asked website chatbots at 3 AM. No judgment."
- LinkedIn: "Your website is working while you sleep. Here's what people are asking at 3 AM." This reframes the humor as a business value prop — your site is always selling, even at 3 AM.
- TikTok: Slideshow format with a lo-fi or "sleepy" trending sound. Each slide is a 3 AM chat. End with "EasyAsk: We answer so you can sleep." The cozy-late-night aesthetic is a whole TikTok subgenre.

---

### Strategy 5: "What the AI Really Wanted to Say" as the Hero

**How it works:** On TikTok especially, flip the format. Lead with the inner monologue line as the hook, THEN show the chat that prompted it.

**Why it goes viral:** The inner monologue is the most shareable single line in each post. Leading with it creates curiosity ("wait, what did the customer ask?"). This is the classic TikTok "wait for it" structure — tease the punchline, then deliver the context.

**Execution:**
- TikTok: Text on screen: *"They asked one word. I wrote four paragraphs. This is fine."* [beat] Then the chat scrolls in. The disconnect between the AI's composure and its inner monologue is the comedy.
- X: Post just the inner monologue line as a standalone tweet. No image. Let it get engagement on its own. Then reply with the full chat mockup an hour later. Two impressions from one piece of content.
- LinkedIn: Use the inner monologue as the opening line (the "above the fold" hook before "see more"). People click to see what prompted it.

---

### Strategy 6: Duet/React Bait for TikTok Creators

**How it works:** Design certain posts specifically to invite reaction content. The "happy snails" post, the "cousin with a nice camera" post, the "does this actually do anything or is it expensive pee" post — these are all duet magnets.

**Why it goes viral:** TikTok creators in those niches (skincare, photography, supplements) will duet the video with their own reaction or hot take. Every duet exposes EasyAsk to that creator's audience. The original content is designed to be incomplete without the creator's reaction — that asymmetry drives engagement.

**Execution:**
- Post the chat mockup video and explicitly invite duets: "Skincare people — duet this with your honest reaction."
- The content should be accurate enough that experts nod along ("the snail answer is actually correct") but funny enough that they can't resist adding their take.
- Monitor which posts get dueted/stitched and produce more content in those verticals.

---

### Strategy 7: Vertical-Specific Account Takeovers

**How it works:** For one week, hand the Typing... format to a specific niche. "This week, Typing... is all pet stores." Five straight days of pet store chats. Promote it to pet industry communities.

**Why it goes viral:** Concentrated vertical content builds community. Pet store owners start following for the pet content, then stay for the rest. It also makes the content easy to share in niche Slack groups, Discord servers, subreddits, and Facebook groups.

**Execution:**
- Pre-announce the takeover: "Next week is Pet Store Week on Typing.... If you've ever had a customer ask your pet store chatbot something unhinged, DM us."
- Cross-post to relevant subreddits, Facebook groups, and niche communities (with mod permission where needed).
- After the week, compile the best 3 into a TikTok/Reel carousel.

---

### Strategy 8: The Rivalry Format

**How it works:** Pair two chats side by side. Same question, two very different businesses. "Someone asked a luxury watch brand AND a $5 Temu store the same question."

**Why it goes viral:** Comparison content performs extremely well on all platforms. The contrast between how two different businesses' AI handles the same question is inherently entertaining and demonstrates EasyAsk's versatility.

**Execution:**
- X: Two images side by side, or a thread with both.
- TikTok: Split screen or quick cut between the two. "Same question, two very different websites."
- LinkedIn: "A $200K enterprise SaaS and a one-person Etsy shop both got asked 'does this actually work?' Here's how each AI handled it." This version emphasizes that EasyAsk scales across business sizes — a B2B angle.

---

### Strategy 9: Seasonal & Trending Moments

**How it works:** Create timely Typing... posts tied to holidays, cultural moments, product launches, and trending topics. "What people ask flower shop chatbots on February 13th." "What people ask mattress store chatbots on Black Friday at 2 AM."

**Why it goes viral:** Timely content gets algorithmic boosts on every platform. The specificity of the date makes it feel urgent and real. People share it because it's relevant RIGHT NOW.

**Execution:**
- Pre-build a calendar of tentpole dates: Valentine's Day, Black Friday, Mother's Day, Back to School, Tax Day, New Year's resolutions, etc.
- Each gets a vertical-appropriate chat: flower shops for Valentine's, fitness for January, tax software for April, etc.
- Post day-of for maximum relevance.

---

### Strategy 10: The Reply Guy Strategy (X-specific)

**How it works:** When business owners or SaaS founders tweet about funny/weird customer behavior, reply with a Typing... mockup for their vertical (not their specific business — keep it fictional). "You think that's bad? Here's what a chatbot on a plumbing site dealt with at 2 AM."

**Why it goes viral:** This inserts EasyAsk into existing viral conversations. The reply adds value (it's funny and relevant), not spam. If the original tweet has 10K impressions, a good reply can ride that wave. Over time, this builds the association: "weird customer questions = EasyAsk."

**Rules:**
- Only reply to tweets that are genuinely about customer behavior or website questions — stay on-topic.
- The mockup should be for a similar-but-fictional business, never a direct mockup of their company (that would be presumptuous).
- Don't hard-sell in the reply. Let the content speak. If they ask "what is this?", then explain.
